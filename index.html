About the route

Mystery without horror — history you can actually enjoy

The route is built around mystical tourism — the lightest shade of dark tourism: castle legends, not sites of tragedy. We don't lean into fear or focus on death — we show how military history, architecture and Belarusian folklore intertwine across a single district.

The tour starts and ends in Smorgon. The bus runs a loop through Krevo, Boruny and Golshany and returns to town by evening. The format suits an organised school group with accompanying teachers just as well as a family travelling independently.

  • Schools & teachers. A ready-made programme for form periods, history and local-studies lessons — grades 8–11.
  • Families. A weekend format without complicated logistics: one bus, one day, a clear programme.
  • Tour operators. A packaged product with timing, points of interest and a workshop — ready to add to a catalogue.
Guiding principle of the route: we present ghosts and legends as part of cultural heritage — the same way we'd present a fresco or a coat of arms — not as horror.

        The concept

        What is mystical tourism?

        Dark tourism means travelling to places connected with tragic or historically significant events. Within that broad field, mystical tourism occupies its own niche: it turns not to death but to legend — the ghosts and curses that have lived around castles and churches for centuries.

        Researcher Philip Stone describes dark tourism as a spectrum of "shades" — from the darkest sites of genuine tragedy to the lightest, heritage-and-entertainment-oriented destinations. Mystical tourism sits toward that lighter end: a long historical distance, an emphasis on heritage, and minimal emotional pressure on the visitor.

        Why this works for schools

        • Legends become a reason to study architecture, heraldry and local history — a topic for discussion, not a source of anxiety.
        • Wartime and memorial episodes are framed as the history of memory, without graphic detail.
        • The "poster" format teaches visitors to tell documented fact from folk legend — that's media literacy in itself.
        In a survey of pupils at our school, mystical tourism came out as the most popular direction of dark tourism — ahead of military, cemetery and other formats.
